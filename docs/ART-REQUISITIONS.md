# Art requisitions

Art Riven needs and does not paint itself comes from Riven Resources, a separate art project
(16 × 16 native textures, `riven` namespace, the library's review standard), fabricated by its
Asset Factory. From Requisition 08 on, every set is drawn in Survey Plate (Riven Resources'
`docs/ART-DIRECTION.md`) and passes its acceptance tests. Riven registers the blocks and places
them; the sets imported so far sit under `tools/content/riven-resources`, and later sets arrive
by Riven's art pack, not this repository (DECISIONS.md, 2026-11-01). Each request names what a
set holds and what it is for; open requests stay here until a set arrives.

## Open

Requisition 08: Survey Plate, the whole game in one language. Riven's blocks come from seven
productions drawn as careful naturalism, and every vanilla block it places or players build with
is vanilla's. This requisition redraws all of it in Survey Plate
(Riven Resources' `docs/ART-DIRECTION.md`): the 565 vanilla textures listed in its
`docs/art/vanilla-blocks.json` as a separate resource pack, Riven Survey
Plate, and the 3,693 resources received from Riven Resources (Decoration Library 01 576,
Requisitions 02 160, 03 176, 04 566, 05 264, 06 193 and 07 1,758). Requisition 07's subjects
stand as requested; where its format notes and ART-DIRECTION.md differ, ART-DIRECTION.md wins.
Items, mobs, the GUI and the sky stay vanilla.

Riven Resources gates this production itself, by the calibration and acceptance tests in
ART-DIRECTION.md; its style guide and worker briefs follow ART-DIRECTION.md from here on. Riven
would use the sets in order: request 46 first; then 47 and 56 for what Riven already places (the
45 vanilla textures flagged `placedByRiven`, and the imported host stones, deposits, realm rocks,
woods and ground cover), so a world reads as one language early; then 48 to 55 and the rest of
56; request 57's maps arrive with the textures they belong to.

Format: native 16 × 16 faces in the palette of Riven Resources'
`docs/art/survey-plate-palette.json`, each with the test kind its inventory entry or source set
names; a main face and an alternate for every block face that tiles; vanilla's sizes, frame
counts, frame timing and UV layouts kept exactly for vanilla paths (block entity sheets, the
32-wide flowing fluids, animated strips); received sets' resource IDs, forms, faces, joins,
alternates and frames kept exactly, so Riven reimports by replacing bytes. Every native texture
passes Riven Resources' `tools/survey_plate.py check`, alternates with `--alternate`, ore and gem
overlays with `--host` over each host the set names.

Requisition 09: cave decorations Riven places without art. When Biomes O' Plenty's blocks left

### 58. Stonebell

`riven:blocks/decor/stonebell_<0-3>`: four forms of a small cave flower that roots in cracks of
bare stone in living depth biomes, crossed planes like Requisition 07's cave life: one to three
thin stems, each bending over in a crook under a single pale, nodding bell. It grows in twilight
and dark, so its bells may carry a faint cool tint but give no light.

### 59. Hanging roots

`riven:blocks/decor/roots_<0-3>`: four forms of roots hanging from the ceiling of a shallow cave
under living ground, crossed planes anchored at the top edge and fading out downward, fine root
threads and a few thicker roots, in the family of Requisition 05's root threads and Requisition
04's drowned roots.

Requisition 10: drift layers. Minecraft 1.12 has partial-height ground only as snow layers, so a
slope of sand or ash steps a full block every few blocks and a walker jumps up each one. Riven
will top drifting ground with layer blocks one to eight eighths tall, read from the column's own
height (traversal proposal T6, 2026-11-17), so slopes under about half a block per block walk
without a jump. Snow uses vanilla's layers; these sets give sand and ash the same. Drawn in Survey
Plate under the same tests as Requisition 08.

### 60. Sediment layers

`riven:blocks/terrain/drift_<kind>_top` and `riven:blocks/terrain/drift_<kind>_side`, main and
alternate each, for three kinds: `sand` (matching vanilla sand as Requisition 47 redraws it),
`red_sand` (matching red sand) and `ash` (matching Riven's `volcanic_ash`). A layer block
crops its faces as vanilla's snow layer does: the top face tiles at every height, and the side is
cut from its bottom edge upward to the layer's height, so the side must read at two pixels tall
as well as at sixteen. A layer resting on a full block of its own kind shows no seam: the top
matches the full block's top, and the side continues the full block's side, with no rim or
outline at its upper edge. No inventory icon beyond vanilla's snow-layer form is needed. Riven registers the
blocks; the art arrives by the pack.

Requisition 11: rock faces. Riven colours each bed of a cliff from its rock unit, and gives every
unit a weathered and a stained state (`riven-geology.json` tint weathering) that stand in with
vanilla terracotta, smooth sandstone and stone until these sets arrive; boulders and the coming
loose stones take the rock under them. Drawn in Survey Plate under the same tests as Requisition 08,
each face matching the host stone it weathers from (Requisition 06, as 56 redraws it) so a fresh
bed, its rind and its stain read as one rock.

### 61. Weathering states

`riven:blocks/rock/<unit>_weathered` and `riven:blocks/rock/<unit>_stained`, main and alternate
each, for gneiss, mica schist, granite, dolerite, limestone, dolostone, shale, sandstone, red beds,
banded ironstone and basalt, plus `evaporite_weathered` (salt crust) and `kimberlite_weathered`
(the yellow ground of a weathered pipe). Weathered is the unit's skin a few blocks under the sun:
paler, its grain opened and its edges softened (granite toward pinkish grus, shale toward flaky
grey clay, basalt and dolerite toward rust brown, ironstone toward red). Stained is the same rock
under a vertical streak of desert varnish or water staining: dark manganese or iron film running
top to bottom, so stacked blocks join into one streak with no seam at the block edges; side faces
only, the top as the weathered face. Stains hang one block wide, 4 to 20 blocks long, so the
side face must read as a single streak and as its broken lower end. The arid bed colours
(`<unit>_bed_<colour>`: shale in six, red beds in five, limestone, dolostone and basalt in two,
evaporite in one) stand in with terracotta today; each wants its unit's grain in that colour,
so a banded wall reads as shale or red beds rather than as clay.

### 62. Dark rock faces

`riven:blocks/rock/<unit>_face` for the eight layered host stones, main and alternate: the shadowed
face of a sheer wall in that rock, darker than the side face by about one value step, with
the bedding running horizontally and the joints vertical, so a wall tens of blocks tall reads as
rock in shadow rather than a lit block repeated. It also stands for the fresh, unweathered
surface of a cut or a fall.

### 63. Loose stones and slabs

`riven:blocks/decor/stones_<unit>_<0-3>` and `riven:blocks/decor/slab_<unit>_<0-1>` for the
eleven units of request 61: four forms of a scatter of fist- to head-sized stones lying on the
ground (a flat model one to three pixels tall, like Requisition 03's frost-heaved stones), and two
forms of a thin flat slab of the same rock (half a block across, two to four pixels tall), each in
its unit's weathered colours with darker undersides. They lie where soil is thin on stony ground,
50 to 100 times as many as boulders, and on talus under the wall they came from.
